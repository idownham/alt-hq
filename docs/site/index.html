The four projects

What each one is testing

All four use Kalshi, a legal U.S. market where people buy "yes" or "no" on real events. A "yes" pays $1 if the event happens and $0 if it doesn't. So a 40¢ price means the crowd thinks it's about a 40% chance.

Project 1 · repo alt-arb

Osprey

Finished: no edge

Are the same games ever priced differently on two markets, enough to lock in a guaranteed profit?

Like two stores selling the same item. If one store sells "Team A wins" and the other sells "Team B wins", and you can buy both for less than $1 total, you win no matter who wins the game.

  1. 1 · Build the price checker
  2. 2 · Collect 2+ weeks of data
  3. Verdict: stop

Result (Oct 28)

15 days, 6,494 rounds of price checks on up to 361 games, 8 leagues. 0 real opportunities by the rules set in advance. The biggest gap before a game started was worth $2.33, and the bar was $20. No money was risked.

Fine print

The checker took 1.5–9 minutes per round, so very short gaps could be missed. Fewer games were covered in the last days, and there was a 1-hour outage on Oct 22. All of these could only hide gaps, not invent them. They're written down next to the verdict.

What happens next

Nothing more to build. The robot is still running. You decide whether to switch it off.

How it was decided

A "real" opportunity had to show up before the game starts, last for at least two price checks in a row, and be worth at least $20 after fees. Go at 5 or more a week, stop below 1 a week. Result: 0 a week, so stop.

Project 2 · repo alt-scalp

Coin Toss

Enough recordings, analysis overdue

Right around kickoff, do prices wiggle back and forth enough for a patient "shopkeeper" to earn the gap between buying and selling?

A shopkeeper posts a buy price (48¢) and a sell price (51¢) and earns the 3¢ difference each time someone trades both ways. The danger: the one time a team scores, everyone trades with you at the wrong price.

  2. S1 · Record games
  3. S2 · Measure the wiggles (overdue)
  4. S3 · Simulate the shopkeeper

Another 168 weren't clean, mostly because the laptop slept.

Early signal (practice games only)

In the October practice games the buy/sell gap was only 1¢ and the typical game had 1 wiggle, against a bar of 3. Not a conclusion: the counted games haven't been analyzed.

How it gets decided

Project 3 · repo alt-btc15

Slot Machine

Recording on the server

Kalshi runs a new "Will Bitcoin be up or down?" market every 15 minutes. Is there a way to beat it?

Round 1 tried to beat it by grabbing prices that were already posted. That failed. Round 2 tries the shopkeeper approach instead: post your own prices and wait for people to come to you.

  1. S1 · Test 4 strategies on past data
  2. Result: none worked
  3. R1 · Record live order books
  4. Shopkeeper test (after 900 usable windows)

Result so far (finished)

12,690 past 15-minute markets, 61 strategy versions, final test run once. All four strategies lost money or broke even per trade (A −$0.81, B −$1.18, C −$0.27, D −$0.13). No edge. No money was risked.

What happened

On the laptop, the share of each day recorded swung from about 1% to 98% (Oct 19 – Nov 8) because it kept sleeping. A window only counts if 95% of it was recorded, so the rules pushed the end date back. Recording moved to the cloud server tonight.

What happens next

Keep recording until there are 900 usable windows (about 10 days of nearly full coverage). Then run the shopkeeper test exactly as written in advance.

How it gets decided

At least 900 usable 15-minute windows. The first two-thirds are used to choose a strategy, and the last third tests it once. It passes only if the test makes money and we're 95% sure that isn't luck. Even a pass doesn't allow trading. That would need its own decision.

Project 4 · repo alt-edge

Bloodhound

Ready to start

"Will Trump say ___ in his speech?" markets. Does doing your homework (old transcripts, the fine print) beat the crowd's gut feel?

Unlike the others, this is a homework race, not a speed race. If Trump has said "tariff" in 9 of his last 10 rallies, and the market prices it at 60¢, the homework says that's too cheap.

  1. S0 · Research + set the rules
  2. S1 · Build the predictor (not started)
  3. Score on past speeches
  4. Paper trades

Biggest finding

These markets close the moment the word is said, so the final price already "knows" the answer. Comparing against it would be unfair. The test instead uses the price 15 minutes before the speech starts.

Scale

The Trump market is huge: 6,858 settled questions across 241 events since April 2025, about 16 events a month. Vance markets are scored too, as a side result.

What happens next

Its turn came when Osprey stopped on Oct 28, but no work has been done since Oct 14. The Oct 25 and Nov 8 targets for it passed without a predictor.

How it gets decided

The homework model's probabilities must be measurably more accurate than the market's price 15 minutes before each speech, with 95% confidence, on speeches it never saw while being built. Only the Trump result decides pass or stop.

Plain-English terms

Words you'll hear in the project chats

Prediction market
A market where you buy "yes" or "no" on an event. The winning side pays $1.
Edge
A reason you'd win more than you lose over many bets, after fees. The whole portfolio is a search for one.
Spread
The gap between the best buy price and the best sell price. You pay it whenever you trade right away.
Maker vs. taker
A taker accepts a price that's already posted (fast, higher fees). A maker posts their own price and waits (the "shopkeeper"; lower or no fees).
Arbitrage
Buying both sides on two markets for less than $1 total, so you profit whatever happens. Osprey's idea.
Adverse selection
The shopkeeper's risk: people trade with you right when they know something you don't, like just after a goal.
Pre-registration
Writing the pass/fail bar down before seeing results, so you can't move the goalposts.
Backtest
Testing a strategy on past data to see how it would have done.
Train / test split
Pick the strategy using older data, then check it once on newer data it never saw. This catches strategies that only fit the past.
Paper trading
Pretend trades with no money, tracked as if they were real.
Order book
The full list of everyone's posted buy and sell offers at each price.
Brier score
A grade for how accurate probability guesses are. Lower is better. Bloodhound has to beat the market's grade.
